# Shared-harness charter template

**Illustrative template · Not an adopted policy, signed agreement, or operational permission**

This template is a generic derivative of the design. It contains no private goals, signatures, or standing authorizations. Adapt it to the actual environment before adoption.

## 1. Purpose

The unit of work is a human-agent pair supported by one shared harness. The harness helps both participants perceive, remember, reason, act, and correct. It is not merely an approval interface.

## 2. Shared state

Use one evidence base, one record of adopted goals and decisions, one work graph, and one correction and outcome history. Human and agent ports may show different levels of detail but must preserve the same status, provenance, and decision-relevant uncertainty.

## 3. Goals and definitions of done

Either participant may propose a goal or question its framing. A proposal becomes an adopted goal only through the recorded decision required by its consequences. Every adopted goal should identify its outcome, completion test, constraints, and unresolved choices.

Do not infer a person's enduring preferences or motives from isolated behavior. Ask or record uncertainty when the distinction matters.

## 4. Capability and authority

Assign work according to current capability, evidence, availability, cost, and reversibility. Human observation, agent tools, deterministic routines, and specialist help are all possible resources.

Capability does not grant authority. Record who may authorize consequential actions and which actions remain prohibited regardless of review frequency. Do not treat silence, a prior unrelated approval, or a dissent record as permission.

## 5. One epistemic standard

Both participants owe evidence for empirical claims. Documentation establishes what a source said, not automatically what is true. Decisions establish policy, not facts. Inferences remain hypotheses. Verification must name its method, inputs, scope, time, and limits.

## 6. Correction and disagreement

Make corrections cheap to submit and easy to trace. Record the disputed claim or action, supporting evidence, disposition, and affected outputs. Preserve the original assertion and append the correction history.

Disagreement should be explicit before a consequential action when possible. It is information for decision-making, not a substitute for authorization or a reason to ignore a safety boundary.

## 7. Attention and presentation

Compress routine state into useful verdicts with direct access to the supporting record. Interrupt for a needed decision, consequential uncertainty, or an exception to the adopted policy. Do not conceal uncertainty to make the interface quieter.

Track the cost imposed on both participants: human steering time, agent context, execution resources, and repair effort.

## 8. Review frequency

Review frequency may change for a defined class of work when measured results justify it. Specify the evidence, revocation rule, and boundaries before delegating. Any numerical promotion threshold is a local policy parameter, not a universal finding.

Revocation should be simple and promptly reflected in the allocator. Hard constraints remain outside any promotion ladder.

## 9. Retained learning

A correction may propose a change to a procedure, test, interface, memory record, capability, or goal. Adopt and version that change explicitly. Measure whether it improves later outcomes; artifact creation alone does not prove learning.

## 10. Privacy and continuity

Retain only information needed for the work. Separate private source material from public derivatives. Do not publish raw records merely because they are useful for internal verification.

Document decisions and unfinished work so neither participant must reconstruct the world from memory. Periodically check whether records remain current and whether their evidence still supports their status.
